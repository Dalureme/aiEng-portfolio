AI Engineering
as a career path

This is a plan to go from high school to building real AI projects and aiming for University of Toronto. It keeps the skills practical: coding, math, data, and teamwork.

Teacher: Mr. Wagner Goal: U of T Target: AI Engineering

Big idea

AI engineering is building software that learns from data. It is not just coding, and it is not just math. It is both, plus real-world problem solving.

Focus: Canada and Toronto region job data.

What you build

  • Apps that classify, predict, or recommend
  • Systems that use models safely and reliably
  • Tools that turn messy data into decisions

What is AI engineering?

AI engineering is taking machine learning and making it work in real life: real users, real data, real failures, real deadlines.

The pipeline

  • Collect data, clean it, label it, store it
  • Train a model and measure accuracy and errors
  • Ship it into an app and monitor it over time
  • Fix problems: bias, bad data, bugs, drift

Reality check: You do not need to invent new AI. Most jobs are about using AI correctly and safely.

Skills map

AI engineering is a stack. If one layer is weak, everything above it gets shaky.

Core layers

  • Python: main language for ML and data
  • Math: functions, vectors, probability
  • Data: spreadsheets, SQL basics, cleaning
  • ML basics: training, testing, overfitting
  • Software: Git, debugging, teamwork

Shortcut that is not a shortcut: Build small projects weekly. Skills grow faster when your brain has a reason to care.

Weekly practice plan

  • 3 coding sessions (45 to 90 min)
  • 2 math sessions (30 to 60 min)
  • 1 project build session (2 hours)
  • 1 reflection: what broke, what you fixed

Project ideas

  • Spam detector using text features
  • Image classifier with a small dataset
  • Study tracker that predicts test scores
  • Music recommender using playlists

High school plan

U of T CS expects strong math. Your job is to keep doors open by choosing the right courses.

Courses to prioritize (Ontario)

  • MCV4U (Calculus and Vectors)
  • MDM4U (Data Management) recommended

Strategy: Take the hardest math you can handle and keep your grades high. Challenge plus consistency.

University of Toronto CS
what they ask for

Computer Science is a direct path into AI engineering. Engineering, Math, and Stats can also lead there.

Key facts

  • OUAC admission code: TAD
  • Prerequisites include English and Calculus
  • Supplemental application is required
  • Supplemental has 4 short answers
  • No deferral for the CS admission category

What this means: Grades matter, and your writing matters. Show proof you can learn, build, and stick with hard stuff.

How to stand out

  • One bigger project with a real problem
  • Clear write-up: what failed, what changed
  • Evidence of improvement over time
  • Teamwork or leadership example

Experience that counts

For AI engineering, experience is proof. Proof beats vibes.

Portfolio checklist

  • 8 to 12 small projects (fast to review)
  • 2 medium projects (full app, clean design)
  • 1 big project (real users or real data)
  • Each project has a short write-up and results
  • Clear demo steps so it can be tested

Rule: every project answers: what problem, what data, what model, what result, what next.

AI projects that look strong

  • Image classifier with confusion matrix and errors explained
  • Text classifier with precision and recall
  • Recommendation system with offline evaluation
  • Simple chatbot with guardrails and logging
  • Data pipeline that cleans and validates messy inputs

Skills that matter

  • Explaining decisions clearly
  • Working in a team without drama
  • Documenting so others can run your code
  • Fixing bugs fast

Paid experience
co-op and internships

Paid work terms give experience, references, and a resume that gets interviews.

U of T PEY Co-op (Engineering)

  • Up to 20 months paid work experience
  • Usually 12 to 16 months after third year
  • Optional 4 months after second year

Source: U of T Work Integrated Learning page.

Why it matters: internships separate you in a crowded market.

How to land internships

  • Build 2 projects that match real job postings
  • Write a 1 page resume with measurable results
  • Apply weekly and track everything
  • Do mock interviews and fix weak spots
  • Ask for referrals when you have proof projects

A timeline
that works

Small steps stacked. No magical motivation required.

Next 90 days

  • Week 1 to 2: Python basics and Git basics
  • Week 3 to 4: Data cleaning project with real CSV
  • Week 5 to 6: Simple ML model and evaluation
  • Week 7 to 8: Build a small web app demo
  • Week 9 to 12: Upgrade, document, publish

Fast win: finish one project to a clean, shareable state. That beats ten half-finished ideas.

Final deliverables

  • Repo with README
  • Demo video (60 to 120 seconds)
  • Results with numbers
  • Limitations with honesty
  • Next steps list

One sentence you can use

“I build AI features end-to-end: data, model, evaluation, and integration into an app.”
